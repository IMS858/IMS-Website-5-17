The order is the method.

Assess, prepare, load, recover. Each stage produces the information the next one needs, which is why none of them get skipped.

Book the free assessment

Stage 01 · Assess

We measure active range, not passive flexibility.

Anyone can be pushed into a position. The question is what you can get into and control on your own — and the gap between those two numbers is usually where the trouble is.

Joint by joint

We work through the joints systematically rather than chasing whatever hurts. A restricted hip changes what the low back has to do; assessing in isolation misses that.

Active versus passive

Range you can produce yourself is range you can use under load. Range that only appears when someone pushes you into it is not yet yours.

A baseline you keep

The assessment becomes the reference point. Progress is measured against your own starting numbers, not against a population average or an age chart.

Stage 02 · Prepare

Every session opens with joint work.

Controlled Articular Rotations — slow, deliberate rotation taken to the edge of what you control, one joint at a time. It is the least entertaining part of the hour and the reason the rest of it holds up.

  • Slow enough that you cannot use momentum to cheat the range
  • Taken to your end range, not to a range borrowed from someone else
  • Done before load, every session, without exception

Stage 03 · Load

Strength is built inside the range you own.

Once a joint can produce and control a range, load makes that range durable. Loading a range you do not yet control is how people end up back where they started.

  • Exercise selection follows your assessment, not a template
  • Volume and intensity adjust to what you are actually recovering from
  • Progression is earned by control, not by the calendar

Stage 04 · Recover

The adaptation happens after you leave.

Training is the stimulus. What you do in the following forty-eight hours decides what you keep from it — so that window gets coached too.

Managing the load

Two hard sessions stacked back to back produce less than two well-spaced ones. Scheduling is part of the program, not an afterthought.

Sleep and consistency

The least glamorous variables move the needle most. We talk about them plainly rather than selling around them.

Where the method comes from

Functional Range Conditioning.

FRC is a joint-health and mobility system developed by Dr. Andreo Spina. Its central claim is that usable mobility is not passive flexibility — it is active, controlled range that you can produce and hold under load. Training it means building strength at the end ranges of a joint, not just stretching into them.

Jason holds FRC, FRA, Kinstretch, and FRC-ISM certifications. The IMS Method applies that framework inside a general strength program, which is why the assessment comes first and the barbell comes fourth.
